## Redundancy

### Useful overlap

- **Global wires and broadsheets:** Reuters, AP, Bloomberg, FT, The Economist, NYT, Guardian, BBC, DW, France 24, Al Jazeera. Overlap is intentional for cross-checking, but it is not 12 independent observations of the same event.
- **US/UK:** NYT, WSJ, NPR, Politico, Foreign Policy, The Guardian, BBC, FT, The Economist, Middle East Eye. This is the densest cluster; it should be weighted down when measuring “global” salience.
- **Middle East:** Al Jazeera, Arab News, Middle East Eye, Times of Israel, +972, Gulf News, Hürriyet, Al-Monitor. The apparent depth is partly a set of opposing national and ideological lenses, not eight neutral observers.
- **South Asia:** The Hindu, Indian Express, Scroll, Dawn, Daily Star, Dhaka Tribune, Prothom Alo, TBS. Bangladesh has four profiles, which is useful for domestic depth but disproportionate relative to population and feed viability.
- **Climate:** Mongabay and Carbon Brief are complementary, not redundant: one is conservation/forest reporting, the other climate policy and science.

### False redundancy

- Reuters and AP are both unavailable, so their profiles do not currently add feed capacity.
- Bloomberg has two feeds but one publisher; the registry counts two rows, not two independent publishers.
- El País English is a curated translation of a Spanish newsroom, not a separate reporting operation.

## Gaps

1. **Mainland China** — no mainland Chinese English-language publisher. SCMP is Hong Kong-based and Alibaba-owned. This is the largest single coverage gap.
2. **Latin America** — one Argentine English-language publisher (Buenos Aires Times); no Brazilian, Mexican, Andean or Central American national publisher. El País English adds Latin American coverage but is Spain-based.
3. **Africa** — three publishers (South Africa, Nigeria, pan-African aggregator). No francophone, Horn of Africa, Sahel or East African national source.
4. **Central Asia and the Caucasus** — no dedicated source.
5. **Pacific islands** — no dedicated source; ABC Australia is the closest.
6. **Right-leaning and centre-right voices** — six centre-right profiles, no right-wing profile. The portfolio is not politically balanced.
7. **Business/finance depth** — FT, Bloomberg, Economist and Business Standard cover markets, but there is no dedicated emerging-markets or energy-transition source.
8. **Climate adaptation and extreme weather** — Mongabay and Carbon Brief are strong on policy and conservation, but not a global rapid-response weather/adaptation feed.
9. **Media freedom and disinformation** — no dedicated press-freedom or open-source verification publisher.
10. **Languages** — English-only. This is operationally coherent but structurally excludes most of the world’s primary reporting.

## Redundancy control

- Keep one global wire layer, not every wire.
- Add national publishers only when they add a distinct domestic lens.
- Treat multiple feeds from one publisher as one publisher for concentration metrics.
- Track story-level duplication separately from publisher count.
- Do not use the 60-profile count as a measure of independence.

## Recommended next research

Prioritise mainland China, Brazil/Mexico, francophone Africa, Central Asia, a centre-right US/UK publisher, and a climate/energy specialist with a valid feed. Do not add another generalist wire unless it closes a documented gap.
